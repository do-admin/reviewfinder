Q: What exactly does this platform offer for school assistance in Hanover?

Q: Does school assistance also cover terms like integration helper or participation assistant?

Whether school companion, integration helper, or inclusion worker – the designations merely vary depending on the federal state or funding agency. The actual task of supporting your child individually in class always remains identical. We cater entirely to the needs approved under the term common in your region.

Q: How do I find a suitable assistant for my child through this site?
A: You can easily describe your situation to us and submit a request. We then carefully match your child's individual support needs with our network to find a personally and professionally optimally suited assistant for you.

Q: Do you also help with questions regarding the application for the measure?

We do not leave you alone with the processes and explain the necessary steps as well as the exact responsibilities. This way, you know exactly which documents or medical reports are needed so that the approval can proceed as smoothly as possible. In addition, we provide valuable tips for dealing with the involved authorities. This saves you valuable time and nerves.

Q: Who covers the costs for school assistance?
A: As a rule, the costs are fully covered by the responsible integration assistance provider or the youth welfare office. The prerequisite for this is that your child's need for help has been officially determined and approved by a formal notice. Thus, you as parents do not incur any costs of your own for an approved measure.

For example, the accompanying person structures everyday school life, helps with focusing on worksheets, accompanies them to the toilet, or supports communication with classmates. However, they do not replace a teacher and do not give grades. The goal is always to provide help for self-help in order to gradually strengthen your child's independence.

### Basics: Tasks & Terms (Grundlagen: Aufgaben & Begriffe)

Q: What is the difference between a school companion and a school assistant?
A: In practice, these two terms are mostly used synonymously. There is no nationwide, legally binding distinction between the two designations.

Q: What does a school assistant do?
A: A school assistant provides practical support to children with special educational needs in their everyday school life. Tasks include practical life help such as accompanying them to the toilet, assistance with eating, or help with structuring work materials. However, they do not take on any teaching activities of their own and do not give grades. The ultimate goal is always to help the child help themselves, in order to enable the greatest possible independence.

Q: What is an inclusion assistant?
A: In contrast to classic school companions, an inclusion assistant often works not only with a single child, but on a class or school basis. The task is to make the entire school system more inclusive and to relieve teachers in the integration of all students. A specialized pedagogical education is usually required for this responsible role.

Q: What does an integration helper do in kindergarten?
A: An integration helper in a daycare center accompanies children with disabilities in everyday routines and in playing with other children. They support with communication, eating, or dressing, and help the child integrate well into the group structure. This lays an important foundation for a later, successful school career.

Q: Is a learning companion a teacher?
A: No, a learning companion is not a classic teacher in the traditional sense. While teachers plan lessons, impart specialist knowledge, and assign grades, a learning companion acts more as a supporting coach. They create an environment in which children can acquire knowledge independently and develop their individual learning skills. Examining and evaluating functions fade completely into the background.

Q: What are the main goals of individual case assistance?
A: The primary goal of 1-on-1 support is the individual promotion of independence and ensuring the child's social participation. Through targeted empowerment, the child should be enabled to cope with their everyday school life as much as possible without outside help in the long term.

### Personnel, Qualifications & Safety (Personal, Qualifikation & Sicherheit)

Q: Who is allowed to work as a school assistant?
A: Since the job title is not legally protected, there is no mandatory required degree. A lateral entry is absolutely possible, provided the person brings a high degree of empathy, patience, and resilience. However, those who want to care for complex cases usually need training in the social sector, such as a curative education nurse or educator. Ultimately, the provider or the office decides on a case-by-case basis whether the respective qualification is sufficient for your child's support needs.

Q: How long does training as an integration helper take?
A: A classic, state-recognized multi-year training does not exist for this specific professional profile. Internal basic qualifications or certificate courses at private educational institutions usually last between a few weeks and several months.

Q: What is a pedagogical specialist in assistance?
A: This term refers to people who take on supporting tasks in daycare centers or schools, but do not hold a leading position. Often these are trained child care workers or social assistants who work under the close guidance of educators or teachers.

Q: What is Section 72a SGB VIII?
A: This section regulates the legal exclusion from employment of persons with relevant prior convictions in child and youth welfare. Providers may not employ anyone who has been convicted of offenses against sexual self-determination. To ensure this, all care workers are required to submit an extended certificate of conduct.

Q: What does Section 8a SGB VIII state?
A: Section 8a anchors the state's mandate to protect in the event of an imminent threat to a child's welfare. It obliges pedagogical professionals and authorities to immediately assess the risk if there are indications of neglect or violence. If parents are unwilling or unable to avert the danger, the youth welfare office or the family court must be involved. This gives families the certainty that all actors must look closely and take action.

### Entitlement, Application & Legislation (Anspruch, Beantragung & Gesetzgebung)

Q: Who is entitled to an integration helper?
A: Children and adolescents who have or are at high risk of a physical, mental, or psychological disability are entitled. This includes, among others, diagnoses from the autism spectrum, extremely pronounced ADHD, or genetic conditions such as Down syndrome. The decisive factor for the authority is always that this individual support is absolutely necessary to make attending school or daycare possible in the first place.

Q: How do I apply for an integration helper?
A: The application must generally be submitted by the parents with custody. Depending on the type of disability, either the local youth welfare office is responsible for psychological disabilities, or the social welfare office for physical and mental disabilities. You should attach medical or child and adolescent psychiatric reports to the application that prove the exact need. An additional statement from the school is often very helpful to illustrate the specific need for help in everyday school life.

Q: How long does an application for integration assistance take?
A: The processing time usually varies between a few weeks and several months, depending on the region and the complexity of the case. Legally, however, the authorities must clarify the fundamental responsibility within two weeks of receiving the application.

Q: How do you write an application for integration assistance?
A: An application for integration assistance is not legally bound to a strict format and can theoretically even be submitted informally. However, it is strongly recommended to use the official forms of the responsible funding agency right from the start to avoid delays. It is important to describe your child's limitations in detail and to attach copies of all existing medical reports. The specific goal of the assistance, such as successful participation in lessons, must be formulated crystal clear.

Q: Who approves integration assistance?
A: The final approval and issuance of the decision is always carried out by the responsible rehabilitation provider. Depending on the federal state and the type of disability, this is the local social welfare office, the youth welfare office, or a so-called supra-local provider.

Q: What is the overall planning procedure in integration assistance?
A: This procedure is the central instrument of the authorities to determine a person's individual need for assistance in a structured manner. It ensures that all required measures are planned precisely and ideally approved from a single source. Parents, assessors, and involved offices are firmly integrated into this process. At the end is the completed overall plan, which forms the binding basis for the subsequent benefit decision.

Q: What does Section 35a of the SGB VIII regulate?
A: This extremely important section regulates integration assistance specifically for children with an impending or existing mental disability. It applies, for example, to severe forms of ADHD, autism, or extreme learning disabilities if participation in society is massively impaired as a result. For parents, this is often the most important legal basis for financing school accompaniment through the local youth welfare office.

Q: What is an assistance plan according to § 36 SGB VIII?
A: The assistance plan is a binding document created by the youth welfare office in close cooperation with the parents and professionals. It records the identified needs, the exact pedagogical goals, as well as the type and duration of the assistance in writing. This plan is jointly reviewed at regular intervals and adapted to your child's current development.

Q: When is the "Big Solution" SGB VIII coming?
A: The so-called "Big Solution" (Große Lösung) plans to bundle the official responsibility for all children with disabilities – whether physical, mental, or psychological – under the umbrella of the youth welfare office. According to current legislation, this comprehensive inclusive solution is scheduled to come into force on January 1, 2028. Hopefully, this will save affected families many bureaucratic hurdles and grueling disputes over responsibilities in the future.

Q: Is integration assistance part of SGB 9 or 12?
A: Since the major reform through the Federal Participation Act (BTHG) in 2020, integration assistance has been completely regulated in Part 2 of SGB IX. Previously, it was anchored in SGB XII with classic social assistance for decades.

Q: What benefit groups are there in integration assistance?
A: The law divides integration assistance into four central benefit groups to specifically cover different areas of life. These include benefits for medical rehabilitation, participation in working life, participation in education, and social participation. For a school-age child, "participation in education" is usually the decisive legal area from which the necessary assistance is financed.

Q: What is LVR integration assistance?
A: The abbreviation LVR stands for the Landschaftsverband Rheinland, which acts as a supra-local social provider in large parts of North Rhine-Westphalia. If you live in this region, the LVR is directly responsible for processing and financing participation benefits for certain types of disabilities.

Q: What type and scope of individual case assistance is granted under § 27 SGB VIII?
A: Section 27 forms the legal basis for all general educational assistance that families can claim from the youth welfare office. The exact type and scope of the assistance are never based on fixed templates, but always strictly on the individually determined support needs of your child. This ensures that state care is exactly as intensive as the family situation requires.

Q: What is the legal entitlement to daycare in SGB VIII?
A: In Germany, every child has a legal entitlement to early childhood support in a daycare center or childminding service from the age of one. This entitlement naturally also applies to children with disabilities, which is why appropriate integration assistance can be applied for early on.

### Costs, Responsibilities & Extended Assistance (Kosten, Zuständigkeiten & Erweiterte Hilfen)

Q: Who pays for an integration helper?
A: The costs for approved accompaniment are generally covered by the responsible provider of integration assistance or public youth welfare. As soon as the official approval notice from the office is available, parents do not have to pay for this intensive care out of their own pockets. This applies completely regardless of whether the child attends a regular school, a special needs school, or is still in kindergarten.

Q: What is the difference between integration assistance and social assistance?
A: Classic social assistance primarily serves to financially secure basic living expenses. Integration assistance, on the other hand, is a highly specialized professional service intended to enable people with disabilities to participate in social life in a self-determined manner. Through modern legislative reforms, these two areas have been strictly separated. Parents therefore have absolutely no need to fear being considered classic "social welfare recipients" when they apply for help for their child.

Q: How high is the income contribution for integration assistance?
A: The use of one's own income and assets has been significantly reformed in recent years in favor of families. Benefits for children and adolescents up to the completion of their school education are nowadays usually completely independent of income and assets anyway. Should an official review occur in very special constellations, generous allowances apply. The parents' own income must not legally stand in the way of necessary school inclusion.

Q: What are specialist service hours in integration assistance?
A: This is the official billing unit used by government offices to financially calculate outpatient assistance. Instead of simply transferring a flat-rate sum of money to you as a family, the authority approves a fixed quota of weekly care hours. A commissioned independent provider then bills these provided hours directly to the responsible office later on.

Q: What is financial youth assistance under SGB VIII?
A: This is a special, internal finance department within the youth welfare office that deals exclusively with the accounting implementation of youth welfare services. It calculates any cost contributions, pays the invoices of independent providers, and properly processes the approved budgets.

Q: What is the cost contribution in SGB VIII?
A: Not all youth welfare services are automatically free of charge for families. For certain fully or partially residential measures, the office does indeed check whether parents can be required to make an individual cost contribution. With classic outpatient school accompaniment under Section 35a, however, there is normally absolutely no financial co-payment required from parents.

Q: Which foundations provide money for individual case assistance?
A: If state assistance is rejected or gaps in financing arise, private foundations can step in to provide support. Well-known examples in Germany are "Aktion Mensch" or various regional emergency funds. However, applications for these funds can usually not be submitted by parents themselves, but only through recognized counseling centers such as Caritas or Diakonie.

Q: What types of assistance are there in SGB VIII?
A: The law offers a broad spectrum of measures that support families in crises or with very special needs. In addition to school accompaniment under Section 35a, these so-called educational assistance services include educational counseling, socio-educational family assistance, or partially residential day groups, among others. These diverse offers are intended to ensure that every family receives exactly the support they need in their individual situation.

Q: What is SPFH in SGB VIII?
A: The abbreviation stands for Socio-Educational Family Assistance (Sozialpädagogische Familienhilfe), which represents outpatient educational assistance provided directly in the family's home environment. It helps parents with acute child-rearing conflicts, generally coping with everyday crises, or the sometimes difficult contact with authorities. Very often, it is approved as a valuable supplement to school accompaniment in order to stabilize the entire family system.

Q: What are same-purpose benefits in SGB VIII?
A: Behind this complex administrative term lies the clarification of who has priority to pay when theoretically different agencies could be responsible. If, for example, a medical measure by the health insurance fund and an educational measure by the youth welfare office pursue exactly the same goal, these are called same-purpose benefits. The offices then check extremely strictly whether the health insurance fund or the long-term care insurance fund must step in first. In practice, this unfortunately often means that families have to submit applications to various service providers before the youth welfare office pays.

Q: What is the partnership in upbringing and education in SGB VIII?
A: This term describes the legal and pedagogical requirement that parents, teachers, and school companions must always work together on an equal footing. In practice, professionals are required to fully respect the parents' responsibility for upbringing and to set common goals. A regular, honest, and open exchange is the irreplaceable foundation for functioning school inclusion.

Q: Was genau bietet schulassistenz.online?

Wir begleiten Sie von der ersten Orientierung bis zur finalen Vermittlung einer geeigneten Fachkraft. Q: Deckt die Schulassistenz auch Begriffe wie Integrationshelfer oder Teilhabeassistenz ab?

Ob Schulbegleiter, Integrationshelfer oder Inklusionskraft – die Bezeichnungen variieren lediglich je nach Bundesland oder Kostenträger. Die eigentliche Aufgabe, Ihr Kind individuell im Unterricht zu unterstützen, bleibt dabei stets identisch. Wir richten uns ganz nach dem Bedarf, der unter dem in Ihrer Region gängigen Begriff bewilligt wird.

Q: Wie finde ich über diese Seite eine passende Kraft für mein Kind?
A: Sie können uns ganz unkompliziert Ihr Anliegen schildern und eine Anfrage stellen. Wir gleichen den individuellen Förderbedarf Ihres Kindes anschließend sorgfältig mit unserem Netzwerk ab, um eine persönliche und fachlich optimal geeignete Assistenz für Sie zu finden.

Q: Helfen Sie auch bei Fragen zur Beantragung der Maßnahme?
A: Der Behördenweg über Jugendämter oder Sozialämter kann für Familien oft sehr unübersichtlich und kräftezehrend sein. Wir lassen Sie mit den Abläufen nicht allein und erklären Ihnen die notwendigen Schritte sowie die genauen Zuständigkeiten. So wissen Sie genau, welche Unterlagen oder ärztlichen Gutachten benötigt werden, damit die Bewilligung so reibungslos wie möglich erfolgen kann. Zusätzlich geben wir wertvolle Tipps für den Umgang mit den involvierten Stellen. Das spart Ihnen wertvolle Zeit und Nerven.

Q: Wer übernimmt die Kosten für eine Schulassistenz?
A: In der Regel werden die Kosten vollständig vom zuständigen Träger der Eingliederungshilfe oder dem Jugendamt übernommen. Voraussetzung dafür ist, dass der Hilfebedarf Ihres Kindes offiziell festgestellt und per Bescheid bewilligt wurde. Für Sie als Eltern entstehen bei einer genehmigten Maßnahme somit keine eigenen Kosten.

Q: Was macht die Schulassistenz konkret im Unterricht?
A: Die Aufgaben richten sich immer ganz flexibel nach dem individuellen Hilfebedarf Ihres Kindes. Die Begleitperson strukturiert beispielsweise den Schulalltag, hilft bei der Fokussierung auf Arbeitsblätter, begleitet bei Toilettengängen oder unterstützt bei der Kommunikation mit Mitschülern. Sie ersetzt jedoch keinen Lehrer und führt auch keine Notengebung durch. Das Ziel ist immer die Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe, um die Eigenständigkeit Ihres Kindes schrittweise zu stärken.

Q: Wie schnell kann eine Begleitung organisiert werden?
A: Sobald der offizielle Bewilligungsbescheid des zuständigen Amtes vorliegt, starten wir umgehend mit der passgenauen Vermittlung einer Betreuungskraft.

Q: Was passiert, wenn die Chemie zwischen Kind und Begleitperson nicht stimmt?
A: Ein vertrauensvolles Verhältnis zwischen Ihrem Kind und der Fachkraft ist die absolute Grundvoraussetzung für eine erfolgreiche Inklusion. Sollte es menschlich einmal wider Erwarten gar nicht passen, kümmern wir uns selbstverständlich um einen zeitnahen Wechsel. Ihr Kind soll sich im Schulalltag schließlich rundum wohl und sicher fühlen.

Q: Betreuen die Assistenzen auch auf Klassenfahrten oder Ausflügen?
A: Ja, wenn dieser erweiterte Bedarf im Vorfeld vom Kostenträger genehmigt wurde, begleitet die Fachkraft Ihr Kind selbstverständlich auch bei schulischen Ausflügen oder mehrtägigen Klassenfahrten. Schließlich soll Ihr Kind an allen Aspekten des Schullebens gleichberechtigt teilhaben können.

## Lexikon

**Abrechnung (Accounting Statement):** Die Abrechnung umfasst die jährliche Darstellung von Einnahmen und Ausgaben einer Immobilie. Sie ist für Eigentümer und Mieter wichtig, um die Kostenverteilung zu verstehen. Die Abrechnung muss formal korrekt und nachvollziehbar sein.

**Nebenkostenabrechnung (Utility Cost Statement):** Jährliche Aufstellung der den Mietern zugerechneten Nebenkosten. Sie muss transparent und rechtlich korrekt erstellt werden. Enthalten sind u. a. Heizung, Wasser, Müll, Gebäudeversicherung.
